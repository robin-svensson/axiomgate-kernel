# Regulatory basis — verbatim, sourced, and dated

**Read this before repeating any regulatory claim about AxiomGate Kernel anywhere.**

Everything below was retrieved on **2026-09-10**. Article text is quoted verbatim.
Where a claim rests on secondary sources rather than the Official Journal text, it
says so in the row itself. Regulation moves; treat an undated regulatory claim the
same way this project treats an unverified invariant number.

## 1. The obligations AxiomGate Kernel is built against

| Article | Verbatim | What AxiomGate Kernel does about it |
|---|---|---|
| **Art. 12(1)** Record-keeping | "High-risk AI systems shall technically allow for the automatic recording of events (logs) over the lifetime of the system." | `audit.py` — every decision appended to an HMAC-chained log at the point of decision. |
| **Art. 14(1)** Human oversight | "High-risk AI systems shall be designed and developed in such a way, including with appropriate human-machine interface tools, that they can be effectively overseen by natural persons during the period in which they are in use." | `Verdict.ESCALATE` plus `OWNER_MANDATORY_ACTIONS` — oversight is a state the system can be *in*, not a screen a human is asked to watch. |
| **Art. 19(1)** Automatically generated logs | Providers "shall keep the logs automatically generated by their high-risk AI systems, to the extent such logs are under their control", for a period "appropriate to the intended purpose", "of at least six months" unless other Union or national law says otherwise. | Log is an append-only file; retention is the integrator's storage decision. AxiomGate Kernel does not delete. |
| **Art. 26(6)** Deployer obligations | Deployers "shall keep the logs automatically generated by that high-risk AI system, to the extent such logs are under their control, for a period appropriate to the intended purpose of the high-risk AI system, of at least six months, unless provided otherwise in applicable Union or national law". | Same log, deployer side. See the anchoring caveat in [ROADMAP.md](ROADMAP.md) R2 — a retained log still cannot prove its own length. |

### Penalties — the tier that actually applies

This is the row most often got wrong, including by an earlier version of this
project's own README.

| Article | Verbatim | Applies to |
|---|---|---|
| **Art. 99(3)** | "Non-compliance with the prohibition of the AI practices referred to in Article 5 shall be subject to administrative fines of up to EUR 35 000 000 or, if the offender is an undertaking, up to 7 % of its total worldwide annual turnover for the preceding financial year, whichever is higher." | **Article 5 prohibited practices only.** |
| **Art. 99(4)** | "…shall be subject to administrative fines of up to EUR 15 000 000 or, if the offender is an undertaking, up to 3 % of its total worldwide annual turnover…" | Operator obligations — including **Art. 16** (provider) and **Art. 26** (deployer). |
| **Art. 99(5)** | "The supply of incorrect, incomplete or misleading information to notified bodies or national competent authorities in reply to a request shall be subject to administrative fines of up to EUR 7 500 000 or, if the offender is an undertaking, up to 1 % of its total worldwide annual turnover…" | Misleading the authority. |

**The tier that attaches to a missing audit trail is 99(4) — €15M / 3 %, not
€35M / 7 %.** Quoting the headline number for a logging failure is the same class
of error as citing an invariant the model does not define. Do not do it.

## 2. When the obligations apply — and why that changed

**The 2 August 2026 date is no longer correct for high-risk obligations.**

Regulation (EU) 2026/1744, the "Digital Omnibus on AI", amended the AI Act. New
application dates for Chapter III (which contains Articles 12, 14, 19 and 26):

| Systems | New date |
|---|---|
| Stand-alone high-risk, Art. 6(2) / Annex III | **2 December 2027** |
| High-risk embedded in regulated products, Art. 6(1) / Annex I | **2 August 2028** |

**Unchanged, and already in force:**

- **Art. 5** prohibited practices — since 2 February 2025
- **GPAI obligations**, Art. 51–56 — since 2 August 2025
- **Art. 50** transparency duties — since 2 August 2026

### Evidence status of the deferral

**Not primary-verified.** `eur-lex.europa.eu` refused automated retrieval on
2026-09-10 (HTTP 202, empty body, across four endpoints), so the Official Journal
text of 2026/1744 was **not** read directly. The dates above rest on convergent
secondary sources — Gibson Dunn, Cloud Security Alliance, DLA Piper and a
publication-day analysis giving OJ publication 24 July 2026 and entry into force
27 July 2026 — plus the fact that `artificialintelligenceact.eu` now serves the
new dates on its own Article 19 and Article 26 pages while its implementation
timeline page still shows the old ones.

That is strong corroboration, not proof. **Before this is used in a customer
deliverable, one person must open the Official Journal and read it.** Sources
disagree on which recital carries the justification (40 vs 46), so no recital
number is cited here.

### Why it was deferred — and why that matters here

The Cloud Security Alliance reports the Commission's concern that "neither
industry nor the harmonized standards bodies — CEN and CENELEC — would be ready
in time", and that conformity-assessment infrastructure had not matured.

CEN-CENELEC's own page confirms the state: **no harmonised standard has been
cited in the Official Journal**, so no presumption of conformity under Art. 40
exists yet. Its wording is future tense — "Once published in the Official Journal
of the European Union (OJEU), these standards will provide companies with a legal
presumption of conformity" — and **logging is named explicitly** as one of the
areas still in development.

The obligation to log is law. The standard describing what an adequate log looks
like does not exist yet. That gap is not a marketing opportunity to be overstated:
it means an organisation choosing a mechanism today is choosing without a
conformity presumption to hide behind, and will have to defend the mechanism on
its own merits. Being able to state precisely what a log can and cannot prove —
see the truncation section in the README — is the relevant property.
